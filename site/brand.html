L1 R1

Hold L1. Everything changes. Twenty-four controls doing forty-eight things, on a machine that thinks you're using a keyboard.

The wordmark

The rules are the bumpers.

L1 and R1 sit on top of the pad, so the rules sit on top of the letters. The mark is the shape of the hardware, drawn in type, and nothing else.

The left bar is amber because L1 is the layer: the button that changes what every other button means. The right bar stays grey. They are never the same colour, and the bars are never omitted, because without them it's just four characters.

Futura Medium, tracked to −0.02em, bars at 0.075em with a 0.16em gap. Set solid as L1R1, never L1 R1 or L1/R1. Say it "el-one-are-one."

The mark

Four buttons. One per profile.

The wordmark carries the bumpers; the mark carries the face buttons. Between them they describe the whole pad, and the four circles are the same four colours the profiles use, so the logo is also the legend.

Rings above 32px, solid dots below. A 3.6px stroke closes up and turns to mud at favicon size, so the small one is drawn separately rather than scaled down.

Four colours

One per profile, not one per mood.

The pad has four face buttons and you're building four ways to use the machine. The accents aren't decoration, they're an index: the colour on screen tells you which profile is live.

Orange Terminal and agent. The default, and anything currently pressed.
Blue Lightroom and creative apps. Culling, rating, scrubbing.
Green Connected, confirmed, picked. It's alive and it worked.
Red Rejected, unbound, destructive. Never decorative.

Ground

Black, and one step off it.

InkPage ground
ShellPanels, the mark's plate

True black, not near-black: the pad is black, the app runs on top of dark editors, and a washed-out grey ground would read as a compromise. Text is #F4F4F4 rather than pure white, because pure white on pure black glares.

Voice

Say the true thing, plainly.

Yes

"macOS will make you grant two permissions by hand. Nothing can do that for you."

No

"Seamless setup in seconds! Unleash your workflow."

Yes

"Any button in a chord pays a 140ms delay. Pick ones that can afford to feel slow."

No

"Lightning-fast, zero-latency input handling."

—

Name the limit before the feature. Trust comes from admitting what it can't do. Everyone else's page lies about setup.

—

Numbers instead of adjectives. 140ms, 250ms, one dependency, 33 controls. Never "fast", never "powerful".

—

No em dashes, no exclamation marks. Short sentences. A comma is usually enough.

—

Say the name in full. "L1R1", never "L1", never "l1r1" in prose. It's four characters; spell them out.
